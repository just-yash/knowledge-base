# Basic Problems of an Economy
## Introduction to Economic Problems

- **Economic Problem:** The problem of **choice** arising from **unlimited wants** and **limited resources**.
- **Core Cause:** **Scarce resources** with **alternative uses**.
- **Nature of Wants:** Human wants are unlimited, recurring, and differ in urgency.
- **Limited Resources:** Factors of production like land, labor, capital, and entrepreneurship are finite.

---
## Causes of Economic Problems

- **Unlimited Human Wants:** Desire for goods and services never ends.
- **Scarce Resources:** Resources available are insufficient to satisfy all wants.
- **Alternative Uses:** Resources can be utilized in various ways, requiring selection of the **best use**.

## Central Problems of an Economy

### 1. What to Produce?

- **Decision:** Selecting the specific type of goods and services to produce.
- **Quantity:** Determining the volume of production for chosen goods.
- **Focus:** Balancing consumer goods vs. capital goods.

### 2. How to Produce?

- **Decision:** Choosing the **technique of production**.
- **Labor-Intensive Technique:** Using more labor and less capital (suitable for labor-surplus economies).
- **Capital-Intensive Technique:** Using more machinery and less labor (suitable for capital-surplus economies).

### 3. For Whom to Produce?

- **Decision:** Determining the **distribution of output** among different sections of society.
- **Objective:** Ensuring fair distribution or targeting specific income groups (rich vs. poor).

---
# Other Fundamental Economic Issues

- **Full Utilization of Resources:** Aiming to use all available factors of production efficiently to avoid waste. Unemployed resources is a **Dead Loss**
- **Efficient Utilization:** Producing the maximum output with the minimum possible input.
- **Economic Growth:** Increasing the productive capacity of the economy over time to produce more goods and services.

# Production Possibility Curve (PPC)

### Definition and Concept

- A graphical representation showing various **combinations of two goods** that can be produced with available techniques and with given amount of resources.
- **Conditions:** Assumes fixed resources and constant technology.
- **Also known as:** Production Possibility Frontier (PPF) or Transformation Curve.
- **Marginal Opportunity** : $\frac{loss}{gain}$ → loss in production of 1 good / gain in production of other good
### Schedule and Curve
###### Example : 

| Production Possibilities | Butter | Cheese | Marginal Opportunity |
| ------------------------ | ------ | ------ | -------------------- |
| A                        | 0      | 15     | -                    |
| B                        | 1      | 14     | $\frac{1}{1} = 1$    |
| C                        | 2      | 12     | $\frac{2}{1} = 2$    |
| D                        | 3      | 9      | $\frac{3}{1} = 3$    |
| E                        | 4      | 5      | $\frac{4}{1} = 4$    |
| F                        | 5      | 0      | $\frac{5}{1} = 5$    |

![[Pasted image 20260317212340.png]]


### Assumptions

- The amount of Resources are **fixed**.
- Technology remains **constant**.
- Resources are **fully and efficiently employed**.
- Resources are **not equally efficient** in producing all goods.

### Properties of PPC

- **Downward Sloping:** Shows that producing more of one good requires producing less of the other (**opportunity cost**).
- **Concave to the Origin:** Due to **increasing marginal opportunity cost** (or marginal rate of transformation).

## PPC and Economic Concepts

### 1. Scarcity and PPC

- Points **outside** the PPC are **unattainable** with current resources.
- Points **on or inside** the PPC are **attainable**.

###### Example : 
![[Pasted image 20260318012812.png]]
- Point K is unattainable 
- Points {C, E, H} are attainable

### 2. Choice and PPC

- The economy must choose a specific point on the curve based on society's preferences.

###### Example : 
- if we choose point C - more cheese will be produced 
- if we choose point E - more butter will be produced
- at what point on the curve the economy decides to operate depends on taste and preferences of individuals in the economy

### 3. Full and Efficient Utilization

- Points **on the curve** represent full and efficient utilization.
- Points **inside the curve** indicate underutilization of resources.

###### Example : 
- Operating on points C and E → full and efficient utilization 
- Operating on point H → some unemployed resources

### 4. Economic Growth

- An **outward shift** of the PPC indicates economic growth due to an increase in resources or improvement in technology.
