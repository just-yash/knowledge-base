# Matrix

I don’t believe in college,  
I don’t believe in schools.  
They’re just producing laborers,  
They’re just making fools.

What will you do with a degree,  
If you don’t have the knowledge?  
Just some learned-up debris,  
That won’t be useful after college.

The grey hairs will tag this as  
Some nonsense of the Gen-Z,  
’Cause they themselves have  
Been pressed by the control key.

But I decide not to be  
A determinant of this matrix.  
They just teach you unnecessary stuff,  
But you are as useful as the appendix.

Mugging up information,  
Forgetting it after examination,  
Serving degree-less people,  
Making it your occupation.

This matrix is run by people,  
Who themselves broke through.  
Money is all you need to make,  
No matter how you do.

I don’t believe in college,  
I don’t believe in schools.  
They’re just producing laborers,  
They’re just making fools.

---
### Reflection

This poem challenges the blind faith placed in formal education and questions whether institutions truly serve learning or merely sustain a system. It voices a desire to step outside predetermined paths and seek meaning beyond certificates. At its core, the poem reflects a hunger for authentic knowledge and personal freedom in a world that often rewards conformity.
