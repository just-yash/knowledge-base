# His Story

They told him it was his last chance  
To taste life’s sweetness and dance.  
For once he left the house,  
There would be a new freedom found.  
As alluring as it sounds,  
That freedom would be overshadowed  
By the weight of duties tightly wound.  
His life would no longer be his own,  
His laughter fading to monotone.

Wherever he wandered,  
Emptiness clung to his skin;  
No place felt like it belonged within.  
Even home was no refuge—  
Its rooms filled with faces,  
Confined in hollow spaces,  
That wore human masks,  
But inside, they were zombies.

They chewed his grey matter like curry,  
Consuming him without any worry.  
They quelled him until he died inside,  
Dimmed every bit of light he tried to hide.  
They loaded his back with expectation,  
Let him suffer in silent damnation.  
All his feelings—they chopped.  
For every effort, they mocked.

They said he was squandering his life,  
Without pondering the knife  
Their words became inside his mind.  
People had a knack for malice,  
Made him sleep on a bed of ballasts,  
Turned him into a callous.  
They showered him with derogatory remarks,  
Called him incompetent, insubordinate, stark,  
Lame, shameful, out of line,  
As if his existence owed them time.

People lost their humanity,  
Turned into captors of individuality.  
No space felt like home anymore;  
His existence they deplored.  
They devoured all the peace he had,  
Burdened him with duties mad,  
That tightened like a noose of stone  
Each time he returned “home.”

A mountain of unrealistic expectations,  
Too steep for anyone to climb.  
And yet, they demanded he outshine,  
And be better than some golden squire,  
Plucked from others’ tales—  
Manipulated, exaggerated,  
Forever overrated.  
A shadow he could never chase,  
Surreal goals he was made to face.

There was nothing he could do,  
Except try—and fail too—  
Himself and everyone around.  
His worth was lost, never found.  
He became the least valued one,  
Yet bore the weight of everyone.  
No matter what he did or said,  
He was never good enough to them.  
His good was denounced,  
Frivolous—he was pronounced,  
While someone “better” was announced.

And so, happiness  
Was never his to claim.  
“His” people did not realize  
How their actions  
Slayed the man alive,  
Maimed his soul’s humanity,  
Leaving him in insanity,  
Reducing him to a bug, a pawn,  
A toy to break at every dawn.

He was taken for granted,  
His feelings shredded, thrown away,  
So he learned, day by day,  
To caress his head for affection,  
To mimic the warmth and connection  
That no one offered, no one gave—  
The feeling of comfort, calm yet grave.

In time, he became  
An encumbrance by name—  
His needs beneath their wants,  
His silence their riposte.  
A stoic with no desire,  
Every human interaction  
Felt like a deposition.

And when he stopped to reflect,  
Truth struck, cold and direct:  
This emptiness was not born  
When he set foot off his parents’ lawn.  
He had been cooped up all along, unseen.  
It had always been this way—  
From the beginning.

---
### Reflection

This poem exposes the slow destruction caused by emotional neglect and impossible expectations. It portrays how a person can be surrounded by people yet feel profoundly alone, slowly losing their sense of worth and identity. Beneath the heaviness lies a quiet recognition: the pain was never sudden—it was accumulated, and being named is the first step toward reclaiming oneself.
