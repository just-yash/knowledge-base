# The Weight of Yesterday

The past will follow to your grave,  
A shadow you can’t outrun or save.  
It haunts you even on your best days,  
A quiet excuse for others to drift away.  

No matter how much you’ve changed,  
Or how well your words are now restrained,  
The past—engraved like a name on a blade—  
Waits for your silence to misbehave.  

It hurts in ways you can’t explain;  
You’d trade your joy to undo that pain—  
To twist the clock and right the wrongs,  
To mute the echoes that don’t belong.  

But time is cruel; it never bends,  
And some regrets refuse to end.  
So here you stand, still learning to breathe,  
While dragging a ghost no one else can see.  

You can’t store it in a basket;  
It will follow you straight to your casket.  
All the feelings of regret—you try to mask it,  
While pretending to be happy and ecstatic.  

The past is condescending; you can’t fight—  
It turns you stoic, dims your light.  
It lingers, even as you strive,  
Keeping half of you barely alive.  

It’s subliminal to the present tense,  
An uppity voice that makes no sense.  
It makes you wallow in regret and shame,  
Then blames you softly, without a name.  

It stands still like a stone—unmoved and alone—  
No matter how deeply you atone.  
It wraps around you like a noose,  
A silent ache you can’t unloose.

---
### Reflection

This poem portrays the past as an ever-present companion—one that does not fade with time but evolves into a quieter, heavier burden. It captures how regret can coexist with growth, and how healing is not erasure but endurance. Beneath the weight lies a subtle truth: survival itself is a form of resistance.
