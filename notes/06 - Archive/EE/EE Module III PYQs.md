**Perfectly Competitive Market - Definition:**

- A perfectly competitive market is a market where:
    - There are large number of buyers and sellers
    - Price is decided by the demand and supply of the product, which is called equilibrium price
    - No single buyer or seller can influence the price
    - All sellers sell homogeneous products
    - Free entry and exit of firms

**Price Determination:**

- In a perfectly competitive market, price is determined by the interaction of market demand and market supply
- The price at which quantity demanded equals quantity supplied is called the equilibrium price
- The quantity at which this occurs is called the equilibrium quantity

**Equilibrium Condition:**

$$Q_d = Q_s$$​
**Diagram:** 


- Point of intersection of demand and supply curve is the equilibrium point
- At equilibrium: Price = Equilibrium price, Quantity = Equilibrium quantity

**Excess Demand and Excess Supply:**

- If price > equilibrium price:
    - Supply > Demand
    - Excess supply occurs
    - Price will fall back to equilibrium
- If price < equilibrium price:
    - Demand > Supply
    - Excess demand occurs
    - Price will rise back to equilibrium
$$Price \propto \frac{1}{Demand} \propto Supply$$
**Self Adjusting Nature:**

- The market is self adjusting in nature
- Supply and demand are self adjusting in such a way that they tend to reach equilibrium price
- eq price when demand = supply, quantity supply = market equilibrium = market supply
- Perfectly competitive market is a market where price is decided by demand and supply of product
- No perfectly competitive market is found in reality, though it is used as a theoretical concept
- price > eq price → excess supply → excess demand will decrease → price will again reach eq price
