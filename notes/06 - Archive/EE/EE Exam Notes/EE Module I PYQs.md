###### Q1, 4)

| Define law of demand and law of supply. Using appropriate diagrams, explain why the law of demand does not work in case of precious and inferior product ?<br><br>Define the law of demand and law of supply. Using appropriate diagrams, explain how the demand and supply curve illustrates the related laws? | 5 Marks | (2022 Q2a, 2023 Q2a, 2024 Q2a) |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ------------------------------ |
###### A1, 4) 
**1. Law of Demand**
- **Definition:**
    - States that _ceteris paribus_ (other factors constant), **quantity demanded decreases when price increases** and vice versa.
- **Mathematical Form:**
    - $Q_d=f(P)$, where $Q_d∝ \frac{1}{P}$​
- **Key Assumptions:**
    - Income constant
    - Tastes and preferences unchanged
    - Prices of related goods constant
- **Reason:**
    - **Diminishing marginal utility**
    - **Substitution effect**
- Demand Schedule and Demand Curve :
![https://ask.learncbse.in/uploads/db3785/original/2X/8/8ef16dda369dfd6466f381b2d8a32b02e9e0b758.png](https://ask.learncbse.in/uploads/db3785/original/2X/8/8ef16dda369dfd6466f381b2d8a32b02e9e0b758.png)
- **Explanation of Diagram:**
	- **X-axis:** Quantity demanded (Q)
	- **Y-axis:** Price (P)
	- **DD curve slopes downward** from left to right
	- Shows **inverse relationship**:
	    - $P↑⇒Q_{d}↓$
	    - $P↓⇒Q_d↑$

**2. Law of Supply**
- **Definition:**
    - States that _ceteris paribus_(other factors constant), **quantity supplied increases when price increases** and decreases when price falls.
- **Mathematical Form:**
    - $Q_{s} = f(P)$, where $Q_{s} \propto P$
- **Reason:**
    - Higher price → higher profit → producers supply more
- Supply Schedule :

|**Price ($)**|**Quantity Supplied (Units)**|
|---|---|
|1|10|
|2|20|
|3|30|
|4|40|
|5|50|

- Supply Curve 
![[Pasted image 20260324042236.png]]
- **Explanation of Diagram:**
	- **X-axis:** Quantity supplied (Q)
	- **Y-axis:** Price (P)
	- **SS curve slopes upward** from left to right
	- Shows **direct relationship**:
	    - $P↑⇒Q_s↑$
	    - $P↓⇒Q_s↓$

**3. Exceptions to Law of Demand**
(A) Precious Goods (Veblen Goods)
- Examples: Gold, diamonds, luxury cars
- **Behavior:**
    - Higher price → higher demand
- **Reason:**
    - **Status symbol / prestige value**
    - Consumers associate high price with high quality
- **Result:**
    - Demand curve becomes **upward sloping**
**Diagram: Veblen Goods Demand**

![https://cdn.corporatefinanceinstitute.com/assets/veblen-good1-e1677182851557.png](https://cdn.corporatefinanceinstitute.com/assets/veblen-good1-e1677182851557.png)

(B) Inferior Goods (Giffen Goods)
- Examples: Cheap staple foods (rice, wheat in poor economies)
- **Behavior:**
    - Price rises → demand also rises
- **Reason:**
    - **Strong income effect outweighs substitution effect**
    - Consumers cannot afford better substitutes
- **Result:**
    - Demand curve **slopes upward (exception)**
**Diagram: Giffen Goods Demand**

###### A2)    
Individual Demand :   
- Price of the given commodity ($P_x$)
	- $P_{x}\uparrow$ : $D\downarrow$
- Price of related goods ($P_{r}$)
	- price of substitute goods $\uparrow$ : demand of current good $\uparrow$  
		- price of tea $\uparrow$ : demand of coffee $\uparrow$
	- price of complementary goods $\uparrow$ : demand of current good $\downarrow$
		- price of butter $\uparrow$ : demand of bread $\downarrow$
- Income of the consumer ($Y$)
	- income $\uparrow$ : demand $\uparrow$
	- income $\downarrow$ : demand $\downarrow$
- Taste and Preferences ($T$)
	- if some commodity is in trend / fashion : $D \uparrow$
- Expectations of future price change ($F$)
	- if price $\uparrow$ in future : current demand $\uparrow$ 
	- if price $\downarrow$ in future : current demand $\downarrow$ 

Market Demand (individual + the following):  
- Size and Population and composition (age group, ethnicity, etc) ($P_{o}$)
	- population $\uparrow$ : demand $\uparrow$ 
- Season and weather ($S$)
	- in summers demand for winter clothes decreases 
- Distribution of Income ($D$)
	- Equal distribution : demand for essential goods $\uparrow$
	- Unequal distribution : demand for luxury goods $\uparrow$
- government policies ($G$)
	- $\uparrow$ taxation : demand $\downarrow$ 
	- subsidies $\uparrow$ : demand $uparrow$

###### Q3)

| Expansion and contraction of demand curve | 1   | (2021 Q1h) |
| ----------------------------------------- | --- | ---------- |

###### A3) 
1. Definition
- Expansion of Demand:
    - Increase in quantity demanded due to fall in price, ceteris paribus
- Contraction of Demand:
    - Decrease in quantity demanded due to rise in price, ceteris paribus

2. Key Feature
- Both are movements along the same demand curve (DD)
- Caused only by change in price, not by other factors

3. Expansion of Demand
- Condition: $P↓⇒Q_d↑$
- Movement from point A to B (downward movement)
- Also called Extension of Demand

4. Contraction of Demand
- Condition: $P↑⇒Q_d↓$
- Movement from point B to A (upward movement)

Diagram: Expansion and Contraction

![https://dineshbakshi.com/images/stories/economics_diagrams/ext_cont_of_demand_small.gif](https://dineshbakshi.com/images/stories/economics_diagrams/ext_cont_of_demand_small.gif)

- Downward movement: Expansion
- Upward movement: Contraction

5. Difference from Increase/Decrease in Demand
- Expansion/Contraction:
    - Movement along curve
    - Due to price change
- Increase/Decrease:
    - Shift of curve
    - Due to other factors (income, tastes, etc.)

6. Conclusion

- Expansion and contraction explain price effect on demand
- They validate the law of demand (inverse relationship)
- Important for understanding consumer behavior in markets

| Factors affecting supply / law of supply | 5 Marks | (2021 Q2b) |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------- | ---------- |
###### A5)      
individual supply :    
- Price of the commodity ($P_{x}$) : 
	- $P_{x}\uparrow$ : $S\uparrow$
- Price of related goods ($P_{r}$)
	- Price of substitute goods $\uparrow$ : supply of current good $\downarrow$
		- Example: Farmers shift from wheat to sugarcane for better profit 
	- Price of complementary goods $\uparrow$ : suppy of current good $\uparrow$
		- Example : Price of butter $\uparrow$ : supply of butter $\uparrow$ : supply of bread $\uparrow$
- Cost of Production ($P_{f}$ or $P_{c}$)
	- land(rent) , labour(wages) , capital(interest) , entrepreneurship(profit) 
	- Cost ↑ → Supply ↓
	- Cost ↓ → Supply ↑
- Technology ($T$)
	- improved tech $\uparrow$ → higher efficiency → supply $\uparrow$
	- obsolete tech $\downarrow$ → lower efficiency → supply $\downarrow$
- Government Policies ($G$)
	- Tax $\uparrow$ : profit $\downarrow$ : supply $\downarrow$
	- subsidies $\uparrow$ : profit $\uparrow$ : supply $\uparrow$
- Goals of the firm ($O$)
	- Profit maximization : supply $\uparrow$ only if profit $\uparrow$ 
	- Market Share Maximization : supply $\uparrow$ at lower prices to capture the market 
	- Social welfare(NGOs and Govt. agencies) : supply $\uparrow$ regardless of profit

market supply(individual supply + the following) : 
- No. of firms in the market ($N$) : 
	- more firms : more supply 
	- less firms : less supply 
- Future expectations in price change($E$) : 
	- future price $\uparrow$ : supply now $\downarrow$
	- future price $\downarrow$ : supply now $uparrow$
- infrastructure and development ($F$)
	- roads and communication system
	- better infrastructure : supply $\uparrow$
	- poor infrastructure : supply $\downarrow$

| iv  | Calculate the cross elasticity of demand for B when the price of A decreases from Rs.7.50 to Rs6.50, and quantity demand for B increases from 30 to 70 kg. Find out whether A and B goods are complements and substitutes?                | 5     | 2023 Q2b |

###### A6) 
formula of cross elasticity : 
$$E_{d}​ = \frac{\Delta Q_{d}}{\Delta P}​​ \times \frac{P}{Q_{d}}$$
Where:
- $Q_d$ = Quantity demanded
- $P$​ = Price 
- $\Delta Q_{d}$ = Change in Quantity demanded 
- $\Delta P$ = Change in Price

i)    
$E_{d} = \frac{20}{10} \times \frac{40}{100} = 0.8$   
- $E_{d} < 1$ ⇒ inelastic demand  
- $E_d > 0$ ⇒ substitute goods

ii)   
$E_{d} = \frac{10}{0.1} \times \frac{0.5}{100} =0.5$ 
- $E_{d} < 1$ ⇒ inelastic demand  
- $E_d > 0$ ⇒ substitute goods

iii) 
$E_{d} = \frac{20}{0.1} \times \frac{0.4}{100} =0.8$  
- $E_{d} < 1$ ⇒ inelastic demand  
- $E_d > 0$ ⇒ substitute goods

iv) 
$E_{d} = \frac{40}{-1} \times \frac{7.50}{30} =-10$  
- $E_{d} = 1$ ⇒ relatively elastic demand 
- $E_d < 0$ ⇒ complementary goods

###### Q9, 10) 
| Explain the methods of measuring national income | 5 Marks | (2021 Q3b, 2022 Q3a, 2023 Q4a, 2024 Q3b) |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------- | ---------------------------------------- |
###### A9) 
- Definition of National Income
	- National Income = Total value of all final goods and services produced within a country during a year
	- It includes factor incomes earned by residents
	- Formula (basic): $NI = \text{Sum of all factor incomes}$

- Methods of measuring national income: 

1. Product Method (Value Added Method)
	- Meaning: Measures total output produced
	- Formula:  $NI = \sum (Value\ of\ Output - Intermediate\ Consumption)$
	- Steps:
	    - Identify sectors: Primary, Secondary, Tertiary
	    - Calculate value added at each stage
	- Precaution:
	    - Avoid double counting

2. Income Method
	- Meaning: Measures total factor incomes earned
	- Components:
	    - Wages (W)
	    - Rent (R)
	    - Interest (I)
		    - Profit (P)
	- Formula: $NI = W + R + I + P$

3. Expenditure Method
	- Meaning: Measures total expenditure on final goods and services
	- Components:
	    - Consumption (C)
	    - Investment (I)
	    - Government Expenditure (G)
	    - Net Exports (X - M)
	- Formula: $NI = C + I + G + (X - M)$
