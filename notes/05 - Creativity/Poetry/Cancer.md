# Cancer

I’m not doing quite well,  
Dying in this cancerous hell.  
Coughing out blood,  
Loving its taste afterward.

Used to smoke a hundred packets a day,  
Wasn’t able to control the urge anyway.  
Now the doctor says  
Nothing can be done.  
And in my last days,  
I’m being a burden.

Went to the barber to shave off my hair.  
At first, he stared, and then he shaved his own hair.  
Everyone is taking so much care,  
While drowning in an ocean of despair.

Coughing and losing my breath,  
Chemo can only delay death.  
Taking a hundred pills a day,  
Losing muscles and losing weight.

Wish I’d learned my lesson earlier,  
Wish I’d chosen to be healthier.  
Wish I didn’t go for the second burn,  
Should have listened to people’s concern.

---
### Reflection

This poem portrays a body collapsing under the weight of past choices and a mind trapped in irreversible regret. It confronts the slow violence of addiction and the cruel clarity that arrives too late. Beneath the suffering is a quiet warning—small decisions repeated over time can shape endings we never intended to reach.
