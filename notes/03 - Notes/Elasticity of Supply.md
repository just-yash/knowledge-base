# Elasticity of Supply

## Concept of Price Elasticity of Supply ($E_s$)

### Definition and Nature

- **Price Elasticity of Supply** measures the degree of responsiveness of the quantity supplied of a commodity to a change in its own price.
    
- It quantifies how much the supply "stretches" or fluctuates when price levels shift.
    
- **Law of Supply vs. Elasticity**: While the Law of Supply indicates the direction of change (positive relationship), elasticity measures the magnitude of that change.
    

---
## Methods of Measurement

### Percentage Method (Proportionate Method)

- Used to calculate elasticity when changes are given in percentages or absolute units.
    
- **Primary Formula**:
    
    $$E_s = \frac{\% \text{ Change in Quantity Supplied}}{\% \text{ Change in Price}}$$
    
- **Derived (Mathematical) Formula**:
    
    $$E_s = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}$$
    
    - Where:
        
        - $\Delta Q$ = Change in Quantity ($Q_1 - Q$)
            
        - $\Delta P$ = Change in Price ($P_1 - P$)
            
        - $P$ = Initial Price
            
        - $Q$ = Initial Quantity
            

### Geometric Method

- Focuses on the point of origin of the supply curve on a graph to determine elasticity.
    
- No complex calculations; based on visual orientation of the supply curve.

## Revenue and Supply Relationship

### Total Revenue ($TR$)

- **Formula**: $TR = P \times Q$
    
- In numerical problems, if $TR$ is provided, Quantity ($Q$) must be extracted:
    
    $$Q = \frac{TR}{P}$$

## Examples and Solved Problems

###### Example: Basic Calculation

- **Scenario**: Price rises from $10$ to $18$. Quantity supplied rises from $100$ to $140$.
    
- **Data**:
    
    - $P = 10, P_1 = 18 \rightarrow \Delta P = 8$
        
    - $Q = 100, Q_1 = 140 \rightarrow \Delta Q = 40$
        
- **Solution**:
    
    $$E_s = \frac{40}{8} \times \frac{10}{100} = 5 \times 0.1 = 0.5$$
    
- **Result**: $E_s < 1$ (Less than Unitary Elastic).
    

###### Example: Revenue-Based Problem

- **Scenario**: At price $20$, $TR$ is $100$. When price rises to $30$, $TR$ rises to $300$.
    
- **Step 1: Find Quantities**:
    
    - $Q = 100 / 20 = 5$ units
        
    - $Q_1 = 300 / 30 = 10$ units
        
- **Step 2: Apply Formula**:
    
    - $\Delta Q = 5, \Delta P = 10, P = 20, Q = 5$
        
    - $E_s = (5/10) \times (20/5) = 2$
        
- **Result**: $E_s > 1$ (More than Unitary Elastic).
