# Demand
## Definition of Demand

Demand refers to the quantity of a commodity that a consumer is **willing** and **able** to buy at a specific price during a given period of time.

- **Essential Components of Demand:**
    - **Quantity:** The specific amount of the commodity.
    - **Willingness:** The desire to purchase the product.
    - **Ability to Pay:** Having the financial means to purchase the product at a given price.
    - **Price:** The cost of the commodity.
    - **Time Period:** A specific duration (e.g., daily, monthly).

---
# Change in Quantity Demanded v/s Change in Demand

- **Quantity Demanded** refers to a specific amount demanded at a specific price.
- **Change in Quantity Demanded** : Change in demand for a given commodity due to change in its own price

- **Demand** refers to the entire relationship between prices and quantities.
- **Change in Demand** : Change in Demand of a commodity because of factors other than its price

---
## Demand vs. Desire

It is crucial to distinguish between mere desire and economic demand.

- **Desire:** Simply wanting a product without necessarily having the means to buy it.
- **Demand:** Desire backed by the **ability to pay** (purchasing power).
    - _Example:_ A person wanting chocolate but having no money represents desire, not demand.
    - _Example:_ A person wanting chocolate and having money represents demand.

---
## Types of Demand

Demand is categorized based on the scope of the consumer base.

### 1. Individual Demand

- Refers to the quantity of a good that a **single consumer** is willing and able to buy at various prices.

### 2. Market Demand

- Refers to the total quantity of a good that **all consumers** in the market are willing and able to buy at various prices.

---
# Determinants of Individual Demand

Determinants of demand refer to the various factors that influence the quantity of a good or service that consumers are willing and able to purchase at different prices.

---
## 1. Price of the Given Commodity

There exists an **inverse relationship** between the price of a commodity and its quantity demanded.

- **Price Increase(⬆):** Consumers restrict consumption, leading to a decrease in demand(⬇).
- **Price Decrease(⬇):** Consumers are incentivized to purchase more, leading to an increase in demand(⬆).

###### Example
If the price of ice cream increases, consumers will buy less ice cream. Conversely, if the price drops (e.g., during a sale), demand for ice cream will rise.

---
## 2. Price of Related Goods

Related goods are classified into two categories based on how they affect the demand for a given commodity.

### Substitute Goods

Goods that can be used in place of one another to satisfy a specific want.

- **Relationship:** Direct relationship between the price of a substitute good and the demand for the given good.
- **Mechanism:** If the price of a substitute good rises, consumers switch to the given good, increasing its demand.

###### Example
- **Coca-Cola and Pepsi:** If the price of Coca-Cola increases, consumers will buy more Pepsi.
- **Tea and Coffee:** If the price of tea rises, demand for coffee increases.

### Complementary Goods

Goods that are consumed together to satisfy a specific want.

- **Relationship:** Inverse relationship between the price of a complementary good and the demand for the given good.
- **Mechanism:** If the price of a complementary good rises, its demand falls, which consequently reduces the demand for the given good.

###### Example
- **Bread and Butter:** If the price of butter increases, consumers buy less butter, leading to a decrease in demand for bread.
- **Car and Petrol:** If the price of petrol increases, demand for cars may decrease.

---
## 3. Income of the Consumer

The effect of income on demand depends on the type of good.

### Normal Goods

Goods for which demand increases as consumer income rises.
- price ⬆ or income ⬇ demand ⬇
- price ⬇or income ⬆ demand ⬆
- **Relationship:** Direct relationship.

### Inferred / inferior Goods

Goods for which demand decreases as consumer income rises.
- price ⬆ income ⬇ demand ⬆ 
- price ⬇ income ⬆ demand ⬇
- **Relationship:** Inverse relationship.

###### Example
- **Normal Good:** High-quality branded clothing.
- **Inferior Good:** Toned milk (consumers switch to full-cream milk as income increases).

---
## 4. Tastes and Preferences

Demand is heavily influenced by factors such as fashion, customs, and habits.

- **Increased Demand:** If a commodity is in fashion or preferred by consumers, demand rises.
- **Decreased Demand:** If a commodity goes out of fashion or is no longer preferred, demand falls.

---
## 5. Expectations of Future Prices

Consumers' expectations about future price changes affect current demand.

- **Price Rise Expected:** Consumers increase current demand to avoid paying higher prices later.
- **Price Fall Expected:** Consumers decrease current demand, postponing purchases until the price drops.

###### Example
If consumers expect the price of cars to rise in the future, the current demand for cars will increase.

# Summary 
- **Price:** Inverse relationship with demand.
- **Substitutes:** Direct relationship between price of substitute and demand for given good.
- **Complements:** Inverse relationship between price of complement and demand for given good.
- **Income (Normal):** Direct relationship with demand.
- **Income (Inferior):** Inverse relationship with demand.
- **Tastes:** Direct relationship with demand.
- **Future Prices:** Direct relationship between expected future price and current demand.
