# Ethical Hacking
- Job of an Hacker is to reverse engineer systems so that they can know how they work and how they can be broken; the intent determines whether this activity is ethical or malicious.
- Historically, malicious hackers were sometimes called _crackers_, though the term is rarely used in modern cybersecurity discourse.

## Types of Hackers:
### Black Hats / Cracker
> Villain
- **Motive:** Personal or financial gain, disruption, or espionage. These are the "bad guys" or cybercriminals.
- **Methods:** They illegally breach systems to steal data (like credit card numbers or personal info), install malware, hold data for ransom (ransomware), or simply cause damage, pirate stuff, etc.
- **Legality:** Their actions are illegal and malicious.

### Grey Hats
> Anti-Hero
- **Motive:** A mix between white and black. They don't have malicious intent but also don't have explicit permission.
- **Methods:** A grey hat might find a vulnerability in a company's website without permission. They might then report the flaw to the company, sometimes requesting a fee (bug bounty) to disclose the details. If the company doesn't respond or cooperate, a grey hat might publicly release the information about the flaw, which is a key difference from a white hat.
- **Legality:** Their actions are illegal, as they access systems without authorization.

### White Hats / Ethical Hacker
> Hero
- **Motive:** To improve security. These are the "good guys."
- **Methods:** They are hired by companies or work as independent researchers to find vulnerabilities in systems and networks. They report these flaws to the organization so they can be fixed _before_ a malicious attacker finds them.
- **Legality:** Their work is legal and authorized. They are often employed as *cybersecurity professionals*, *penetration testers*, or *security analysts*.

## Work of an Ethical Hacker / White Hat Hacker
- Working with Organizations and Companies to help them fortify and secure their systems.
- Perform various tests including **Penetration Assessment Tests** to provide insight on security capabilities of the organization.
- Create insightful reports and mention every finding and conveying the threat level accordingly.
- Create security policies for the said organization.
- Work in tandem with other departments like Management, PR, HR, etc.
