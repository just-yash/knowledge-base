# Demand Function

The **Demand Function** defines the functional relationship between the **quantity demanded** of a particular commodity and the various factors that influence it.

---
## 1. Individual Demand Function

This represents the demand relationship for a **single consumer** in the market.

- **Formula:** $D_x = f(P_x, P_r, Y, T, E)$
- **Factors:**
    - **$P_x$ (Price of the Commodity):** The price of the specific good '$x$'. Generally, as price increases, demand decreases (inverse relationship).
    - **$P_r$ (Price of Related Goods):** Prices of substitute goods (e.g., tea vs. coffee) or complementary goods (e.g., bread and butter).
    - **$Y$ (Income of the Consumer):** The purchasing power of the individual. Changes in income affect demand depending on whether the good is normal or inferior.
    - **$T$ (Tastes and Preferences):** Consumer preferences, habits, and fashion trends heavily influence demand.
    - **$E$ (Expectations):** Consumer expectations regarding future price changes or availability of the commodity.

---
## 2. Market Demand Function

This represents the total demand for a commodity by **all consumers** in the market.

- **Formula:** $D_x = f(P_x, P_r, Y, T, E, Po, S, D)$
- **Additional Factors (beyond individual factors):**
    - **$P_o$ (Population/Size and Composition):** A larger population generally leads to higher demand. Composition (age structure) also matters.
    - **$S$ (Season and Weather):** Certain goods are highly seasonal (e.g., woolens in winter, umbrellas in rainy season).
    - **$D$ (Distribution of Income):** If income is distributed equally, demand for luxury goods might decrease, while demand for necessities might increase.

---
## 3. Demand Schedule

A **Demand Schedule** is a tabular statement showing the different quantities of a commodity that are demanded at different price levels during a specific period.

### 3.1 Individual Demand Schedule

- Shows the demand behavior of a single consumer.
- Demonstrates that as the price decreases, the quantity demanded increases.

###### Example : 

| Price (in ₹) | Quality demanded of commodity x (in units) |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| 5            | 1                                          |
| 4            | 2                                          |
| 3            | 3                                          |
| 2            | 4                                          |
| 1            | 5                                          |

### 3.2 Market Demand Schedule

- Shows the total demand behavior of all consumers combined.
- Tabular statement showing various quantities of a commodity that all the consumers are willing to buy at various levels of price, during a given period of time
- Derived by horizontally summing the individual demand schedules of all households in the market.

## 4. Demand Curve

The **Demand Curve** is the graphical representation of the demand schedule, showing the relationship between price and quantity demanded.

### 4.1 Individual Demand Curve

- A graphical plot of the individual demand schedule.
- It slopes **downward from left to right**.
- The downward slope indicates an inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

![[Pasted image 20260318152627.png]]

### 4.2 Market Demand Curve

- A graphical plot of the market demand schedule.
- It is the horizontal summation of all individual demand curves.
- Like the individual curve, it is downward-sloping.

![[Pasted image 20260318152745.png]]

- $D_A →$ Individual Demand Curve of Household $A$   
- $D_B →$ Individual Demand Curve of Household $B$  
- $D_M →$ Market Demand Curve  : $D_M = D_A + D_B$
