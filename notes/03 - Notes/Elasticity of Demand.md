# Elasticity of Demand / Demand Elasticity / Elasticity

- **Elasticity of Demand** measures the **responsiveness of quantity demanded** to changes in price, income, or price of related goods.
- It provides a **quantitative measure of demand**, unlike the qualitative nature of the [[Law Of Demand]].
- It indicates the **degree of change in demand**, not just the direction.
- Concept of Elasticity of Demand was developed by Prof. Marshall in his book 'Principles of Economics'
- It refers to the percentage(%) change in demand for a commodity with respect to percentage(%) change in any of the factors affecting demand for that commodity.

$$\text{Elasticity of Demand} = \frac{\text{Percentage change in Demand for X}}{\text{Percentage change in a factor affecting the Demand for X}}$$

---
# Types of Elasticity of Demand

1. **Price Elasticity of Demand**  
   Responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in the **price of the commodity itself**.

2. **Income Elasticity of Demand**  
   Responsiveness of quantity demanded to a **change in consumer income**.

3. **Cross Elasticity of Demand**  
   Responsiveness of quantity demanded of one good to a **change in the price of another good**([[Demand#2. Price of Related Goods|related goods]]).

---
# Price Elasticity of Demand

## Definition
- Degree of responsiveness of demand for a commodity with reference to change in the price of such commodity
- Price elasticity of demand is the **ratio of percentage change in quantity demanded to percentage change in price of the commodity**.

- Establishes quantitative relationship between price and quantity demanded
- except price, other factors remain constant
- higher the value of elasticity, larger the effect of change in price on the quantity demanded
- Elasticity of Demand is always -ve because of inverse relationship 
- Don't consider the -ve sign 
- More the Elasticity, more the effect of change in price on change in the quantity demanded 

---
## Formula

### Percentage Method / Flux Method / Proportionate Method / Mathematical Method

$$E_d = \frac{\text{Percentage change in Quantity Demanded}}{\text{Percentage change in Price}} =\frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta P}$$
where : 

1. **Change in Quantity ($\Delta Q_d$):**$$\Delta Q_d = Q_1 - Q$$_(Where $Q_1$ is the New Quantity and $Q$ is the Initial Quantity)_
    
2. **Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded:**    $$\%\Delta Q = \frac{\Delta Q}{Q} \times 100$$
3. **Change in Price ($\Delta P$):**    $$\Delta P = P_1 - P$$
    _(Where $P_1$ is the New Price and $P$ is the Original Price)_
    
4. **Percentage Change in Price:**   $$\%\Delta P = \frac{\Delta P}{P} \times 100$$
- Elasticity of Demand is always $-ve$ because of inverse relationship
	- if $\Delta{P}$ is +ve then $\Delta{Q_d}$ is -ve 
	- If $\Delta{Q_d}$ is +ve then $\Delta{P}$ is -ve  
- Hence we don't consider the -ve sign generally. 

---
### Derivative Mathematical Form 

By substituting the components back into the core formula, the equation can be simplified for direct calculation:
$$E_d = \frac{\frac{\Delta{Q_d}}{Q_d}×100}{\frac{\Delta{P}}{P}×100}=\frac{\Delta Q_d}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q_d}$$

| Value of $(E_d$) | Type of Goods | Nature           |
| ---------------- | ------------- | ---------------- |

| $( E_d < 0 )$    | Complements   | Inverse relation |
| $( E_d = 0 )$    | Unrelated     | No relation      |
| $( >1 )$         | Elastic       | Strong response  |
| $( <1 )$         | Inelastic     | Weak response    |

# Income Elasticity of Demand

Income elasticity measures how demand changes with **changes in consumer income**.

$$
E_y = \frac{\%\Delta Q_d}{\%\Delta Y}
$$

Where  

- $Q_d$ = Quantity demanded  
- $Y$ = Consumer income  

Interpretation:

- $E_y > 0$ → **Normal goods**  
- $E_y < 0$ → **Inferior goods**

---
# Cross Elasticity of Demand

Cross elasticity measures how demand for one good changes when the **price of another good changes**.

$$
E_{xy} = \frac{\%\Delta Q_x}{\%\Delta P_y}
$$

Where  

- $Q_x$ = Quantity demanded of good X  
- $P_y$ = Price of related good Y  

Interpretation:

- $E_{xy} > 0$ → **Substitute goods**  
- $E_{xy} < 0$ → **Complementary goods**

# Factors Affecting Price Elasticity of Demand

### Nature of Commodity
- **Necessities** → Demand tends to be **inelastic**.
	- ###### Example : Food, Medicines 
- **Comfort** → Demand tends to be **elastic**.
	- ###### Example : Washing Machine, Sofa, Washing Machine
- **Luxuries** → Demand tends to be **more elastic than Comfort**.
	- ###### Example : Gold, Silver, AC, Car

---
### Availability of Substitutes
- More substitutes → **Higher elasticity** 
	- ###### Example : Variety of biscuits in the market
- Fewer substitutes → **Lower elasticity** 
	- ###### Example : Railways

---
### Income Level of Consumers
- Higher income groups → **Less elastic demand**
- Lower income groups → **More elastic demand** 

---
### Price Level of the Commodity
- Expensive goods → **Elastic demand** 
	- ###### Example : If a car gets expensive, the sales fall
- Cheaper goods → **Inelastic demand** 
	- ###### Example : If salt gets a little expensive, its still affordable

---
### Urgency of the Commodity 
- Less urgent → **more elastic demand** 
- urgent demand → **less elastic demand** 

---
### Number of uses 
- several uses of commodity → **more elastic demand** 
	- ###### Example : Milk
- fewer uses of commodity → **less elastic demand** 
	- ###### Example : Petrol

---
### Proportion of Income Spent
- Large proportion of income → **Elastic demand**
- Small proportion of income → **Inelastic demand**

---
### Habit-forming Goods
Goods like tobacco and alcohol generally have **inelastic demand**.

# Summary

- Elasticity of demand measures the **responsiveness of demand** to changes in economic factors.
- Price elasticity of demand is calculated as the **percentage change in quantity demanded divided by percentage change in price**.
- Demand may be **perfectly elastic, perfectly inelastic, unitary elastic, elastic, or inelastic**.
- Elasticity depends on factors such as **nature of the commodity, availability of substitutes, income level, price level, and proportion of income spent**.
