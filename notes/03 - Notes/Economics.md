# Economics 
- Economics is the study of how human beings make choices regarding the allocation of **scarce resources** to satisfy **unlimited wants**.

#### Objectives : 
- **Consumers:** Maximize satisfaction level.
- **Producers:** Maximize profits.
- **Society:** Maximize social welfare.

---
# Nature of Engineering Economics
Economics is characterized by its multifaceted approach to studying human behavior and resource management. It is defined by the following characteristics:

- **Social Science**
     - Studies **human behavior** and economic activities.
     - Observes, collects data, and analyzes patterns to formulate **theories and laws** (e.g., Law of Demand, Law of Supply).
- **Focus on Scarcity**
     - Addresses the fundamental problem of **unlimited human wants** versus **limited resources**.
    - Analyzes how to satisfy needs efficiently with available resources.
- **Art**
     - Involves the **practical application** of economic principles and theories to solve real-world problems.
     - Requires judgment, intuition, and creativity from managers to apply laws in different situations.
- **Positive Science**
     - Deals with **"what is"** by examining facts and **cause-and-effect relationships**.
     - Analyzes how variables interact, such as the impact of price changes on demand.
- **Normative Science**
     - Deals with **"what ought to be"** and involves value judgments.
     - Proposes solutions, policies, and goals to solve economic problems (e.g., reducing unemployment).

# Scope of Economics

The scope defines the area of study and the specific activities covered within economics. It is divided into two main branches:

---
## Microeconomics
- study of small or single units within the economy
- often referred to as a **microscopic study** of the economic system.
- Studies **individual economic agents** and their decision-making processes.
- **Individual Units:** Focuses on firms, consumers, households, and specific products.
- **Subject Matter:** Primarily deals with **price determination** and income distribution among individual economic agents.
- Analyzes:
     - Consumer behavior and utility.
     - Market structures (e.g., perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly).
     - Product pricing and price discrimination.
     - Producer behavior and equilibrium.
 - **Key Topics:**
    - Income of a household.
    - Pricing of a specific firm.
    - Wages of a specific worker.
    - Profit of an entrepreneur.
- **Methodology:** Analyzes how individual consumers allocate income among different goods to maximize satisfaction, and how firms decide on pricing and production levels.
### Alternative Names
- **Price Theory**
- **Value Theory**
### Core Assumption
- Assumes that **macroeconomic variables** in an economy remain constant.

---
## Macroeconomics
- deals with economic activity as a whole, focusing on the entire economic system rather than individual parts.
- Examines the **overall economy** and aggregate behavior.
- **Aggregates:** Studies broad economic aggregates and systemic issues.
- **Subject Matter:** Primarily deals with **income determination** and employment levels for the nation.
- Analyzes:
     - National Income, GDP, and GNP.
     - Inflation and unemployment levels.
     - Business cycles and economic growth.
     - Monetary and fiscal policies.
- **Economic Activities**
     - Covers the production, exchange, distribution, and consumption of goods and services.
- **Key Topics:**
    - **Aggregate Demand** and **Aggregate Supply**.
    - Total consumption and total output.
    - Total employment levels.
    - General price level (inflation/deflation).
    - **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)**.

### Alternative Names

- **Aggregative Economics**

### Core Assumption
- Assumes that the allocation of resources, distribution of output, and relative prices are given.

---
## Microeconomics v/s Macroeconomics 

### Fundamental Differences

- **Level of Analysis:**
    - **Micro:** Individual level (study of trees).
    - **Macro:** Countrywide/systemic level (study of the forest).
- **Focus:**
    - **Micro:** Price determination and income of households/firms.
    - **Macro:** National income, total employment, and total output.
- **Tools:**
    - **Micro:** Demand and Supply of a particular commodity.
    - **Macro:** Aggregate Demand and Aggregate Supply of goods and services.
- **Subject Matter**
     - **Micro:** price 
     - **Macro:** income
- **Perspective:**
    - **Micro:** Bottom-up view of the economy.
    - **Macro:** Top-down view of the economy.
