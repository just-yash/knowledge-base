# How to Read a Paper
>Three-pass Method 

- Reasons to read paper : 
	- conference or a class
	- keep current in their field 
	- literature survey of a new field 
- Typical researcher spends hundreds of hours every year reading papers 
- Read the paper in up to three passes 
	- [[#1st Pass]] : general idea 
	- [[#2nd Pass]] : grasp paper’s content 
	- [[#3rd Pass]] : paper in depth

---
## 1st Pass

- Quick scan 
- Get a bird’s eye view of the paper 
- You can decide whether you need more passes 
- 5 to 10 minutes 
- Steps 
	1. Read the title, abstract and introduction 
	2. Read the section and sub-section headings, ignore everything else 
	3. Read the conclusions 
	4. Glance over the references : mentally ticking off the ones you’ve already read 
- Qs you should be able to answer after the 1st Pass : the five C’s 
	1. Category : What type of paper is it? 
		- A measurement paper?
		- An analysis of an existing system?
		- A description of a research prototype? 
	2. Context : 
		- Which other papers is it related to? 
		- Which theoretical bases were used to analyze the problem? 
	3. Correctness : 
		- Do the assumptions appear to be valid? 
	4. Contributions : 
		- What are the paper’s main contributions? 
	5. Clarity : 
		- Is the paper well written? 
- Use this information, to decide if you want to read further or not 
	- Maybe it doesn't interest you 
	- You don't know enough about the area to understand the paper 
	- Author’s take invalid assumptions 

> When you write : 
>- Choose coherent section and sub-section titles 
>- write concise and comprehensive abstracts 

---
## 2nd Pass 

- Read with greater care 
- ignore details such as proofs 
- helps to jot down the key points 
- make comments in the margins as you read 
- Steps : 
	1. Look carefully at the figures, diagrams and other illustrations 
		- Special attention to graphs 
			- Are the axes properly labeled 
			- Are results shown with error bars → conclusions are statistically significant 
		- Common mistakes like these separate rushed, shoddy work from the truly excellent 
	2. Mark relevant unread references for further reading 
		- good way to learn about the background of the paper 
- 1 hour 
- You should be able to grasp the content of the paper after this stage 
- Be able to summarize the main thrust of the paper with supporting evidence to someone else 
- This level of detail is appropriate for a paper in which you are interested but doesn't lie in your research speciality 
- Sometimes you still don't understand the paper 
	- Subject matter is new to you → unfamiliar Terminology and acronyms 
	- Author may use a proof or technique you don't understand 
	- poorly written paper → unsubstantiated assertions and numerous forward references
	- late night tired reading 
- Options : 
	1) set paper aside : hoping the paper is irrelevant to your career 
	2) return to the paper later : perhaps are reading background material 
	3) persevere and go on to the 3rd Pass
