# The Tragic Downfall of Café Coffee Day (CCD)

---
## The Disappearance of VG Siddhartha

- **Sudden Disappearance:** VG Siddhartha, founder of India's largest coffee chain, went missing on July 29, 2019, after instructing his driver to drop him off at a bridge over the Netravati River near Mangalore.
- **The Suicide Note:** A note was found in which Siddhartha admitted to severe pressure and mounting debt, declaring himself a failure as an entrepreneur despite building a vast empire.

## Rise of the Coffee King

- **Roots in Coffee:** Siddhartha came from a family with a long legacy of coffee cultivation in the Malnad region of Karnataka.
- **Early Interest in [[Marxism]]:** Despite his family's wealth, he was influenced by Marxism and the poverty of plantation workers during his college days, aspiring to be a "Robin Hood" figure.
- **Entry into Business:** He began his career in stockbroking rather than the family business, establishing Sivan Securities and gaining a significant financial boost by investing in _Infosys_ early on.
- **Liberalization of Coffee Industry:** Post-1992, the Indian government eased regulations, allowing planters to sell directly, leading Siddhartha to establish **Amalgamated Bean Coffee Trading Company (ABCTL)** in 1993.
- **Market Dominance:** ABCTL became a major exporter, maximizing profits for local farmers and becoming a price setter in the industry.

## Creation of the CCD Brand

- **The Café Concept:** Inspired by international coffee chains and a desire to create a space for social interaction and internet access, Siddhartha launched the first **Café Coffee Day (CCD)** on Brigade Road, Bangalore, in 1996.
- **Initial Expansion:** The chain was an instant hit, offering Italian-style coffee to a demographic that had limited experience with it.
- **Operational Restructuring:** Under CEO Naresh Malhotra, the company shifted from a familial business to a professional structure, closing unprofitable outlets and implementing financial discipline before aggressive expansion.

## Expansion and Financial Woes

- **Diversification:** CCD expanded rapidly across India, reaching 560 outlets by 2008 and 1700 by the time of Siddhartha's passing. The holding company, _Coffee Day Enterprises_, diversified into real estate, hospitality, and logistics.
- **Mounting Debt:** Rapid expansion was financed through heavy borrowing. Interest costs skyrocketed, and the holding company structure became complex, with profitable units supporting unprofitable ones.
- **Collateralized Shares:** To secure loans, Siddhartha pledged a significant portion of his company shares as collateral.
- **Banking Sector Challenges:** Regulatory changes (Asset Quality Review) reduced the ability to secure new loans to pay off old ones (Evergreening loans).

## The Turning Point: IL&FS Crisis

- **Financial Market Crash:** The 2018 **IL&FS crisis** paralyzed the Indian financial sector, making it nearly impossible for Siddhartha to secure new financing.
- **Desperate Measures:** Siddhartha desperately sought to sell his stake in the IT company _Mindtree_ to repay debts. After intense negotiations, he sold the stake to L&T for ₹3,200 crore, but the debt pressure remained immense.
- **Hidden Liabilities:** Post-death investigations revealed hidden debt burdens totaling approximately ₹3,500 crore.

## Post-Siddhartha: Restructuring and Recovery

- **Leadership Change:** Malavika Hegde, Siddhartha's wife, took over as CEO.
- **Debt Reduction:** The company engaged in aggressive asset sales, including selling the Global Village Tech Park to Blackstone for ₹2,700 crore and shedding unprofitable coffee outlets.
- **Survival:** Through restructuring, the company successfully reduced its massive debt significantly within four years.
