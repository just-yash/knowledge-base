## Early Life and Background

- Born Mumtaz Jahan Begum Dehlavi on February 14, 1933.
- Third child in a conservative Pathan family from Delhi.
- Father, Ataullah Khan, was strict and lost his job at Imperial Tobacco Company due to an outburst.
- Family faced poverty and moved to Bombay in search of work.

## Discovery and Early Career

- Ataullah Khan discovered Mumtaz's talent for singing and dancing.
- Bombay was a burgeoning film city with new studios like Bombay Talkies, founded by Himanshu Rai and Devika Rani.
- Ataullah Khan would take Mumtaz to studios, making her perform in corridors to get noticed.
- Devika Rani discovered Mumtaz, impressed by her expressive face, confidence, and beauty.
- Mumtaz passed a screen test and was cast in the 1942 film _Basant_ at age 8.
- Gained national popularity with a song from the film.
- Became the sole provider for her large family.

## Challenges and Resilience

- In 1944, the Bombay Docks explosion destroyed their home, forcing the family back to Delhi.
- Devika Rani sent a director to bring Mumtaz back for a role in _Jwar Bhata_, though Mumtaz did not ultimately do the film.
- Ataullah Khan realized Bombay was crucial for their livelihood, leading the family to return.
- Appeared in several films as a child artist from 1944 to 1946.
- Director Kedar Sharma, her future mentor, was deeply impressed by her talent and work ethic.
- Unlike many child artists, Mumtaz remained grounded and prioritized her work.
- At age 12, she borrowed 2,000 rupees from a producer to pay for her pregnant mother's urgent medical treatment, as she was only earning 300 rupees a month.
- This incident shaped her generous nature, making her known for being open-handed with money.
- Suffered an injury on set that led to the discovery of an underlying heart condition.
- Devika Rani, believing "Baby Mumtaz" was too common for a star, gave her the screen name Madhubala.

## Rise to Stardom

- Her first lead role came in 1947 in Kedar Sharma's film _Neelkamal_.
	- The film's initial lead actress (Kedar Sharma's wife) died on the day of the muhurat (inaugural ceremony).
	- The producer, Chandulal Shah, was reluctant to finance the film with two newcomers: Madhubala and Raj Kapoor.
	- Kedar Sharma, unwavering, sold his own land to finance the film.
	- Madhubala, then 14, debuted in a lead role alongside 22-year-old Raj Kapoor.
	- Kedar Sharma admired Madhubala's diligence, noting her punctuality despite long commutes by crowded third-class trains.
- On set, a rivalry developed between Madhubala and Raj Kapoor, with Kapoor admitting to being jealous of her ability to outshine him.
- Before _Neelkamal_'s completion, Madhubala's father began accepting other film offers for her, leading to a rift with Kedar Sharma, who felt betrayed.
- The film industry quickly recognized Madhubala as a rising star.
- She continued to transform, impressing veteran actors like K.N. Singh, who noted her change from a child artist to a leading lady.
- Starred in three more films with Raj Kapoor.
- Critics praised not only her beauty but also her versatile acting abilities.

## Personal Struggles and Media Control

- Madhubala's father exerted absolute control over her life, acting like a "military dictator."
    - He managed her entire schedule, from home to studio and back, leaving her no personal time.
    - He was a constant presence on sets, preventing her from forming friendships.
    - Visitors and even cast members needed ID checks to approach her.
- Despite her early successes, Madhubala faced career setbacks, signing many films that flopped.
- The media, which once celebrated her, began to call her "unlucky."
- Her breakthrough came with the 1949 box office hit _Mahal_.
    - This film elevated her from a promising actress to a national sensation, proving her serious acting depth and commercial appeal.
    - Studio management initially resisted casting her due to her "unlucky" reputation and even tried to sabotage her look test.
    - The director, however, personally supervised a second look test, which convinced the studio of her star power.
- Despite her success, she lived simply, avoiding luxury clothes and jewelry.
- She sought personal independence by learning English and driving.

## Relationships and Heartbreak

### Dilip Kumar

- Her romance with Dilip Kumar began in 1951 on the sets of _Tarana_.
- Madhubala initiated the relationship by sending him a rose and a handwritten Urdu note, which he accepted.
- Their nine-year romance was challenged by her father's constant presence, leading to stolen moments on film sets or secret car rides.
- Their on-screen chemistry was widely noted and admired.
- They made a rare public appearance holding hands at a film premiere.
- Dilip Kumar later wrote that she was the only woman who could bring him out of his shell.
- Their relationship ended tragically due to a courtroom battle over the film _Naya Daur_ (1956).
    - Director B.R. Chopra sued Madhubala and her father for breach of contract after Ataullah Khan refused to let her travel for outdoor shooting, citing her health.
    - Dilip Kumar testified in court _against_ Madhubala, stating that her decision violated the contract, despite declaring his love for her.
    - Madhubala was deeply shocked and heartbroken by his testimony, leading to a permanent rift.
    - The film industry was divided, with many criticizing Dilip Kumar for not supporting her.
- Despite their personal estrangement, they had to complete _Mughal-e-Azam_, a film that had been in production for 16 years.
    - Their off-screen romance early in the production fueled their on-screen chemistry as Salim and Anarkali.
    - After the court case, their interactions on set were marked by extreme tension and awkwardness.
    - Madhubala suffered immense physical strain during filming due to her worsening heart condition, often coughing up blood.
    - She performed demanding scenes, like being bound by heavy chains, despite doctors' warnings about the toll on her health.
    - Dilip Kumar would avoid speaking to her, leaving the set immediately after his scenes.
- _Mughal-e-Azam_ became Madhubala's most iconic and successful film, though she was too ill to attend its grand premiere.

### Kishore Kumar

- Met Kishore Kumar in the late 1950s on the sets of _Chalti Ka Naam Gaadi_.
- Kishore Kumar's cheerful temperament helped Madhubala cope with her heartbreak from the Dilip Kumar affair.
- They married in 1960, a decision many viewed as impulsive or a rebound.
- Kishore Kumar was known for his genius but lacked the emotional stability to support a chronically ill wife; he admitted in an interview that he didn't truly love her.
- Madhubala's father never approved of the marriage.
- Her health continued to decline, leading to a trip to London with Kishore Kumar for medical consultation.

## Final Years and Legacy

- Doctors in London diagnosed her with a congenital heart defect (a hole in her heart) that had reached a dangerous stage. Surgery was possible but highly risky.
- Kishore Kumar struggled to handle her worsening condition and emotional distress, often complaining about her.
- Madhubala confided in composer Naushad, who advised her to consider ending the marriage.
- She conveyed her resignation, indicating she was too weary to fight for a new beginning.
- Madhubala eventually moved out, living in isolation in her home, Arabian Villa.
- Her father maintained his strict control, preventing visitors from seeing her.
- Rumors circulated in Bombay about her health and altered appearance.
- She was aware of the rumors, often looking in the mirror and lamenting her decline, stating she didn't want people to see her in that state.
- Her sister described her as a "skeleton."
- She underwent painful procedures, including blood extraction, further weakening her.
- Despite her isolation, she continued to receive fan mail.
- She passed away on February 23, 1969, at the age of 36, just nine days before her birthday.
- In her brief life, she acted in 73 films, with approximately 20 becoming blockbusters.
- Her beauty garnered international attention; legendary Hollywood director Frank Capra wanted to take her to Hollywood, but her father refused.
- An American magazine featured her on its cover with the headline, "The Biggest Star in the World And She’s Not In Beverly Hills."
- Her life, much like her films, was both blockbusting and tragic, yet her work continues to resonate, securing her place as one of India's greatest stars.
