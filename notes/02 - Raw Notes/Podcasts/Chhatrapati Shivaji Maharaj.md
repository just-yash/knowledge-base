# Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj and Secularism
- Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj was "Dharmanirpeksh" (impartial to religions) but not "secular" in the modern sense.
- His administration included a "Dharma Nyay Pandit" position, indicating governance based on "Dharma" (religious law).
- The then-constitution was based on "Dharma," and as a Hindu ruler, he promoted Hinduism.
- Netoji Palkar was reconverted to Hinduism after Aurangzeb had converted him to Islam.
- Historical incidents show conversion of mosques built on temples back into temples.
- Adil Shah's mosques in Kalyan Bhiwandi were converted into granaries, and funding for Maulvis was stopped.
- Afzal Khan and the Badi Begum saw Shivaji Maharaj's expansion as a threat to Islam.
- He did not personally hate other religions but fought for humanity, which is the foundation of Sanatan Dharma.
- Modern secularism is criticized for hiding truth and avoiding uncomfortable facts.
- His army had very few Muslim names; a reward was offered for more than 12 names.
- Muslims working for him were eventually removed after 10-12 years.
- He viewed Muslims as "Turks" or foreign invaders, not Indians.
- An incident in Akola, Maharashtra, where students apologized for enacting a play about Shivaji and Afzal Khan, highlights controversies around historical narratives.
- Different historical narratives taught for convenience divide society and hinder national unity.

# Distinguishing Mughals and Marathas

- The argument that both were simply expanding empires is refuted.
- Mughals were invaders who imposed their rule, surnames, and religion.
- Islamic invaders used force (sword) and economic pressure (Jizya tax) for conversions.
- Jizya tax was designed to impoverish people, forcing them to convert.
- Hinduism, based on "Vasudeva Kutumbakam," accepts people as they are, without conversion.
- Shivaji Maharaj, while not against religion, greatly valued his own religion.
- He did not impose his will on others' homes or cultures, unlike Aurangzeb, who controlled women, gods, and family practices.

# Proofs of Shivaji Maharaj's benevolent leadership:
 
- He encouraged Raja Chhatrasal to establish his own "Swarajya".
	- He returned control of a place to Mallamma Devi in Karnataka after conquering it.
	- He offered to help Mirza Raja Jai Singh conquer all of India.
- The British defeated the Marathas, not the Mughals, indicating the Maratha power.
- Shivaji Maharaj's dream was "Swarajya" (self-rule) for the people.
- He did not name forts or his kingdom after himself, nor did he initially desire a coronation.
- His goal was to live for his people, symbolized by lighting the first Diwali lamp in his name.

- **Kavi Bhushan's Account (Poet of Aurangzeb):**
	- Kavi Bhushan, an Aurangzeb's court poet, sought out Shivaji Maharaj after hearing of him.
	- He wrote poems praising Shivaji Maharaj, calling him a "lion" against "Mlecchas" (foreign invaders/Mughals).
	- Shivaji Maharaj generously rewarded Kavi Bhushan.
	- Kavi Bhushan's poetry suggests that without Shivaji Maharaj, Hindu culture and religion (e.g., Kashi, Mathura) would have been destroyed, and everyone would have converted to Islam.

# **The Obscurity of Shivaji Maharaj's History**
- False claims that Shivaji Maharaj looted Hindu temples have been made.
- These claims are fact-checked and refuted; no "Bakhar" (contemporary historical account) mentions him looting Hindu temples.
- History teaching often contains propaganda rather than accurate "first-hand accounts."
- "Uncomfortable truths" that don't align with certain ideologies are omitted from historical narratives.
- Examples include the omission of casteism, the term "Kafir," or Aurangzeb's destruction of the Kashi temple.
- A fabricated story claims Aurangzeb destroyed the Kashi temple due to alleged kidnapping of Rajput queens by Brahmins, but there is no proof for this.
- Leftist ideologies are accused of using "pen" instead of "gun" to manipulate minds, leading to a lack of nationalism.
- Ramayana and Mahabharata have allegedly been fictionalized, and villains like Ravana and Karna glorified.
- Tipu Sultan is criticized for seeking help from the Ottoman Empire to convert all of India.
- There is a trend to vilify Indian kings and queens while whitewashing the crimes of invaders.
- Historians like Audrey Truschke are accused of whitewashing Mughal actions and downplaying Aurangzeb's temple destruction.
- Mughal records themselves detail temple destruction and their pride in it, which these historians often ignore.
- The downplaying of Mughal crimes and intense search for Shivaji Maharaj's flaws is perceived as a deliberate rewriting of history to hide invaders' genocidal and cultural destruction.
- To learn true history, one should read multiple sources (leftist, rightist) and verify sources.
- Basic facts about Shivaji Maharaj are considered undeniable.
- His history was suppressed because certain historians viewed only Delhi-based rulers as "Sarveshwar" (supreme rulers).
- The concept of "Swarajya" was originated by Shahaji Raja and spread by Shivaji Maharaj, later adopted by figures like Rani Lakshmibai and Lokmanya Tilak in the freedom movement.
- English colonizers believed that if Shivaji Maharaj were born in England, they would have conquered the world.

# **Shivaji Maharaj's Enduring Relevance**

- Shivaji Maharaj's relevance was, is, and will be present as long as there is the moon and sun.
- He guides individuals at different stages of life, especially if one reads his true history.
- **Lessons for different life stages:**
	- **Childhood (0-12 years):** Parents should learn from Jijabai and Shahaji Raje, as children absorb their parents' values.
	- **Youth (16-24 years):** Focus on building good friendships, as an individual is the average of their friend circle. Shivaji Maharaj remembered his loyal "Mawales" (soldiers) like Bajiprabhu Deshpande and Tanaji Malusare.
	- **Collective Consciousness:** Shivaji Maharaj fostered a collective dream, where fighting for "Swarajya" meant protecting one's home, family, and culture. He prioritized duty over personal grief.
	- **Being a Good Citizen:** His dream was a strong nation. Fulfilling duties as a good husband, father, son, and citizen contributes to "Swarajya".
	- **Later Life (40-50+ years):** Individuals should strive to raise children like Sambhaji or Bajirao, or at least good citizens.
- **Three Pillars for Becoming a "Chhatrapati" in one's field:**
	- **Knowledge (Buddhi):** Exemplified by Sambhaji Maharaj writing "Buddha Bhushan". Lack of knowledge leads to historical distortion.
	- **Body (Shareer):** Physical strength protects against external attacks.
	- **Spirituality (Spirituality/Atma):** Jijabai's re-establishment of Dnyaneshwar Mauli's Samadhi symbolized the need to preserve the spiritual soul of the nation.
- Change in societal perception of history is a gradual process, but igniting interest in even a small percentage of people is crucial.
- India will become a nation when its soul is reawakened through figures like Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj.
- Modern leaders are criticized as politicians who prioritize elections over national unity.
- If Shivaji Maharaj were alive, he would first address internal divisions and those who misuse his name for votes but neglect his forts.
- "Swarajya" is a continuous process, not a destination, requiring the participation of every "Mawale" (citizen).
- Teaching Shivaji Maharaj's history is vital for children, parents, police officers, and political leaders to prevent his legacy from becoming merely history.
